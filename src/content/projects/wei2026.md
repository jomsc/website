The Sono ENSTA association (which I was part of at the time) is responsible each year for organizing the party of ENSTA's Integration Weekend in September. The theme for 2026 was "Rio Carnival". Like the previous editions, I handled all the scenography and pre-planning, and we then set up the stage with the other members of the association.

## Preparation

Like the previous editions, preparation began about 3 months in advance (in June 2026). The main student organization asked for a weekend centered around the Rio Carnival, so after some thought, I decided to build something around the idea of a carnival mask. Once again, we would use once again the *Projection Mapping* technique: using a projector to display some colored shapes on a surface. This time, for the 3D structure, I decided to experiment and go with a semi-transparent one, so that we could see the LED screen through it. This required to 3D print the vertices of the structures, and make the edges out of wooden cleats. Then, we used nylon fabric for the faces. The mask was 3D modeled in Blender, and then I made a script to auto-generate the necessary geometry for 3D printing. Here is a screenshot of the mask with the auto-generated geometry. I based my work off of a Aphrodite statue 3D model found on the Musee du Louvre Open Data website.
![Mask 3D model](/images/rio-01.png)

Then, I built the scene around the mask that would be suspended in the air, using the organization's gear, and some that we rented.
![Blender scene general view](/images/rio-02.png)

As stated above, this year with did a semi-transparent structure. We 3D printed the vertices and linked them with wooden cleats, which resulted in a very lightweight structure (less than 3 kg) that was fully capable of holding itself together, and withstanding mild hits. We then glued some semi-transparent fabric on the backside of each face and painted the whole thing black, which looked like this once finished :
![the mask resting on the ground](/images/rio-03.JPG)
